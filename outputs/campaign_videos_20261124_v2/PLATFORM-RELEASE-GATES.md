# Campaign v2 — platform release gates (source check 2026-11-25)

Status: internal operating note only. This is not publication approval and does not replace checking the live upload UI for the account and destination.

## 1. Confirmed policy points

### YouTube
YouTube requires creators to disclose altered or synthetic content that appears realistic or meaningfully changes reality, including making a real person appear to say/do something they did not, altering real-event footage, or generating a realistic scene that did not occur. The creator uses the altered/synthetic or AI-use disclosure during upload. Ordinary minor edits do not automatically meet that threshold. The disclosure itself does not by itself make a video ineligible for monetization.

Official source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491

### TikTok
TikTok requires labeling AI-generated content that contains realistic images, audio or video; it also encourages creators to label fully AI-generated or significantly AI-edited content. A creator can use the AI-generated content setting during upload and/or clear contextual labeling. TikTok may automatically label content using AI signals / Content Credentials. Some deceptive impersonation and other harmful synthetic depictions are prohibited even if labeled.

Official source: https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content

### Instagram / Meta
Meta describes its platform approach as applying “AI info” labels to content it detects as AI-generated or when people disclose AI generation; treatment may differ between fully generated and AI-modified content. Do not assume the same label toggle or workflow across Instagram, Facebook and Threads. Inspect the actual destination's current composer and account settings before posting.

Official source: https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/

## 2. Conservative campaign decision

The three Muse campaign masters are silent, stylized explainer videos, and their current package is still internal review only. We have not established from the manifest alone whether each visual is realistic synthetic footage. Therefore:
- Keep all three videos blocked from public release until a human reviewer checks the full 15 scenes and records whether any scene could be mistaken for real footage.
- If realistic synthetic scenes or meaningful AI alterations are present, enable the platform's disclosure mechanism where applicable and use clear caption context.
- Never imitate a real person's voice/likeness or depict real events deceptively.
- Do not claim YouTube, TikTok or Meta approval; this note is a policy summary, not a platform review.

## 3. Per-post release checklist

- [ ] Full-video human review completed (all scenes, transitions, text, factual claims, mobile safe areas).
- [ ] Source claims checked against primary sources; citations and scope preserved.
- [ ] Asset, font, model and music/voice licenses checked for intended commercial use.
- [ ] No unconsented private-person likeness, voice, personal data or misleading endorsement.
- [ ] AI disclosure decision recorded for this exact cut and destination; upload UI rechecked at posting time.
- [ ] Captions, title, description, hashtags and commercial disclosures reviewed for destination.
- [ ] Destination account ownership and authorization confirmed.
- [ ] HTTPS landing page, free download, privacy notice and paid product terms tested before adding a CTA.
- [ ] Post URL/ID, timestamp/timezone and actual analytics captured after publishing.
- [ ] Orders, refunds, fees and settled proceeds reconciled before reporting revenue.

## 4. Do not infer

- Technical decode success is not editorial approval.

- A draft caption is not a published post.
- Views, likes, clicks, downloads and inquiries are not revenue.
- Unknown platform behavior remains unknown until verified in the current official guidance and account UI.
